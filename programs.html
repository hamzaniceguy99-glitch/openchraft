The programs, in detail

Four programs of 8 to 12 weeks, worked on pieces you finish and photograph.

Program 01 · Buy five things, not fifty

Tools & leather

We start with a tool list that fits a shoebox: a knife, pricking irons, an awl, needles and a burnisher. Then choosing leather — tannage, weight and where to buy offcuts — and cutting to a pattern accurately, which decides whether the rest of the piece can ever be square.

  • 8 weeks
  • Complete beginners
  • 3 hours a week
  • Five tools, some offcuts

Program 02 · The line people judge

Saddle stitching

A saddle stitch is simple and unforgiving. We work on punching at a consistent angle, keeping the same needle order every stitch, and tension that doesn't pull the leather — until the back of your work looks as good as the front, which is the real test.

  • 10 weeks
  • Can cut accurately
  • 4 hours a week
  • Irons and needles

Start this program

Program 03 · Where it stops looking homemade

Edges, dye & finish

Edges separate hobby work from pieces people assume were bought. We cover bevelling, sanding through grits, burnishing with gum or water, and the dyeing process that avoids streaks — including testing on offcuts first, every single time.

  • 8 weeks
  • Stitching confidently
  • 3 hours a week
  • Bevel and burnisher

Program 04 · Things you'll use

Real projects

Each project is chosen to teach something new: a card holder for stitching, a belt for edges and hardware, a wallet for internal construction, and a simple bag for gussets, straps and lining. By the end you can plan your own piece and estimate the leather for it.

  • 12 weeks
  • Basics solid
  • 5 hours a week
  • Leather for one project
